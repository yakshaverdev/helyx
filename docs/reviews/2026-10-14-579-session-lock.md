# Review: #579 One process at a time per session: a writer lock

Invariant: while a session opened with `:sessions_dir` runs, its server process owns a port to a perl program that holds `flock` `LOCK_EX` on `<id>.lock` next to `<id>.jsonl`. The lock is taken in `Helyx.Session.start/2` and `resume/2` before `Helyx.Session.File` writes the session file. A held lock gives `{:error, {:session_locked, id}}` and the file is not written. A failure after the take releases the lock. A stop releases it, or past 1000 ms reports it unconfirmed. A crash or a node halt releases it through the OS. perl ending while the session runs gives one notice, and the session goes on.

Entry points: `Helyx.Session.start/2`, `Helyx.Session.resume/2`, `Helyx.Session.File.pick/3` and `resume_picked/3`, `Helyx.Session.Lock` (`hold/2`, `own/1`, `unlink/1`, `release/1`), `Helyx.Session.Server.start/2` and `init/1`, the `handle_info/2` clauses of the lock port, `Stop.run/1`, `Helyx.Reason.text/1`, `CodingAgent.error_text/1`.

Accepted:

- The lock is not taken again after perl ends (decision 5 of the feature doc).
- `flock` on a network filesystem is out of scope.
- The power-loss guarantee belongs to #584.
- A release past its 1000 ms bound returns unconfirmed. perl is not killed: no evidence shows a real perl that ignores the byte.
- A second open in the same node gets the line "open in another process" (the line of the ticket).

Open race (#589): a caller that another process kills after its unlink of the port, when no `init/1` connects the port later (the request never went out, or the supervisor ends first), leaves the lock held until the node ends. Recorded in the Ownership section of `docs/features/session-lock.md`.
